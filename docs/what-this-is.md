# What Sammati Is and Is Not

## It Is

A small, self-hosted tool that one organisation runs to:

- show people a notice and ask for consent, purpose by purpose
- keep a record of every consent and withdrawal that can be proven later
- let people see and withdraw their consent
- tell the organisation's other systems when consent changes

It was written with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 in
mind, in neutral terms that suit other consent-based laws.

## It Is Not

- **Not a Consent Manager.** Under the Indian law a Consent Manager is a
  company registered with the Data Protection Board of India that lets
  people manage consent across many organisations. Sammati is not that,
  and running it does not make you one.
- **Not a compliance certificate.** Using it does not make you compliant.
- **Not a cookie banner or scanner.**
- **Not a full privacy programme.** No rights-request handling, breach
  reporting, risk register or audits.
- **Not a hosted service.** Nobody runs it for you.
- **Not intended for Significant Data Fiduciaries**, who carry additional
  obligations.

## Who Is Responsible

You are. Sammati is self-hosted software under the MIT licence, provided
without warranty of any kind. The person or organisation that installs it:

- runs and secures the server
- owns the data and the backups
- writes the notices and decides what to ask consent for
- answers to the people whose data it is and to regulators

The author and contributors have no access to your installation and take
no responsibility for it. The setup screen asks you to confirm this before
you can use the tool.

## If You Install It for Clients

The MIT licence lets you install, host, customise and charge for Sammati.
Make sure your client knows who runs the installation and who is
responsible for it, and put that in your agreement with them.
